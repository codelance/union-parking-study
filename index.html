Traffic Engineering & Queuing Theory

Solving 3-Service Turnarounds (8:30 AM • 10:30 AM • 12:30 PM) & Chick-fil-A Drop-Off Flow

How to handle 1,000 attendees per gathering, maintain an always-open exit lane during arrivals, and prevent turnaround gridlock at 9:45 AM and 11:45 AM.

Rule #1 • The Always-Open Escape Lane

Why the S-Curve Throat Can Never Be 100% Inbound

If Ambassador Christian Way is coned 100% Inbound-Only during arrivals, no vehicle can exit the property—trapping parents dropping off youth, Rideshare (Uber/Lyft) drivers, early departures, and the entire 8:30 AM congregation when 10:30 AM arrivals begin showing up at 9:45 AM.

  • • Highway Capacity Manual (HCM) Math: A single unobstructed lane moving continuously at 15 mph without stop signs or backing-out cars carries 1,500–1,800 vehicles/hour (25–30 cars/min).
  • • Dedicated 1-In / 1-Out Throat: Keep the East (Right) S-Curve Lane Inbound and the West (Left) S-Curve Lane ALWAYS Outbound from 7:45 AM to 1:30 PM. Flip to 2 Outbound Lanes only at 1:45 PM after the final 12:30 PM service.
Rule #2 • Passenger-Side Curb Geometry

Running the Curved SE Portico Loop from NE → SW

Because the 2,100-seat auditorium sits Northwest of the curved Portico Loop, entering the Portico Loop from the Southwest puts the building on the driver's (left) side—forcing children to step out into moving traffic.

  • • NE → SW Entry from East Corner (V3): All inbound vehicles turn Right (Counter-Clockwise) at V1. At the East Corner, Drop-Offs peel Left into the Portico Loop traveling NE → SW so the passenger (right) door faces the covered lobby sidewalk.
  • • Two Zero-Conflict Exit Choices at SW End: Unloaded cars either turn Right into Zone A to park on campus OR continue Straight down the West S-Curve Outbound Lane to leave campus immediately.

5 Chick-fil-A Queuing ($M/M/c$) & ITE Land Use 560 Best Practices

1. Upstream Triage (Mobile-Thru Split)

Chick-fil-A separates high-variance orders from fast app orders upstream. By pulling Drop-Offs, ADA, and First-Time Guests (flashers) into the Portico Loop at the East Corner, 80% of general traffic flows straight into Zone B/C at 15 mph with zero stops.

2. Dual-Lane Unload + Escape Bypass

Never unload 1 car at the door while 15 wait behind it. Split the curved Portico drive into an Inner Curb Unloading Lane (6–8 cars unloading at once) + an Outer Chick-fil-A Bypass Lane so Car #5 can pull out as soon as it unloads.

3. Ping-Pong Zone Alternation (B ↔ C)

During the 9:45–10:25 AM and 11:45 AM–12:25 PM swaps, never send arriving cars into an aisle where departing cars are backing out. Park 8:30 AM in Zone B; route early 10:30 AM arrivals (9:45–10:10 AM) past Zone B into Zone C while Zone B flushes!

4. Disney-Style Sequential Loading

An unmanaged lot stalls out at 80% capacity because drivers stop in aisles hunting for spaces. Cone off all inactive rows; direct every car into the active row front-to-back (cutting parking time from 12s to 3s per car).

5. Platoon / Batch Crosswalks (V2)

Continuous pedestrian trickle freezes vehicle loops. Stanchions funnel pedestrians to a single crosswalk at V2 where attendants hold walkers for 45–60 seconds and cross platoons of 30+ people in 15-second windows.

6. Zero-Crossover Ring Circulation

Inbound stays on the East S-Curve and East/SE perimeter; Outbound drains down the West/NW perimeter and West S-Curve. A continuous cone wall through V1 guarantees zero path intersections.
